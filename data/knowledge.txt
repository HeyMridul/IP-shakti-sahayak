SOURCE 1
Title: Guidelines for Examination of Ayush Related Inventions - 2025
Organization: Intellectual Property India
URL: https://ipindia.gov.in/resource/patents-resources-guidelines

Key information:
The Indian Patent Office has published Guidelines for Examination of Ayush Related Inventions - 2025.

These guidelines are relevant to examination of patent applications involving AYUSH-related inventions.

When assessing an invention, patentability requirements must be considered under applicable Indian patent law and examination practice.

A claim involving traditional knowledge should not automatically be treated as a new invention merely because the traditional knowledge has been presented in a different form.

Questions involving Ayurveda, traditional medicine, biological resources, formulations, processes and related inventions may require examination of novelty, inventive step and other applicable requirements.

The official IP India guidelines should be checked for the latest and exact examination position.

SOURCE 2
Title: Traditional Knowledge and Intellectual Property
Organization: World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO)
URL: https://www.wipo.int/en/web/traditional-knowledge/tk/index

Key information:
Traditional knowledge includes knowledge, know-how, skills and practices developed and passed between generations within a community.

Traditional knowledge can include medicinal, agricultural, scientific, technical and ecological knowledge.

Traditional knowledge as such is generally not protected by conventional intellectual property systems in the same way as a new invention.

Innovations based on traditional knowledge may potentially interact with patents, trademarks, geographical indications, trade secrets or other forms of intellectual property protection depending on the circumstances.

WIPO describes defensive protection as strategies intended to prevent third parties from obtaining inappropriate intellectual property rights over traditional knowledge.

Documentation and databases of traditional knowledge can help demonstrate prior art and may assist patent examination.

SOURCE 3
Title: Intellectual Property and Traditional Medical Knowledge
Organization: World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO)
URL: https://www.wipo.int/publications/en/details.jsp?id=4692

Key information:
Traditional medical knowledge can raise intellectual property questions concerning protection, use, commercialization and benefit sharing.

Different intellectual property approaches may be relevant depending on the type of knowledge, innovation and jurisdiction.

Traditional medical knowledge may interact with existing intellectual property systems as well as policy approaches specifically designed for traditional knowledge.

The rights and interests of traditional knowledge holders and communities are important considerations.

SOURCE 4
Title: WHO International Standard Terminologies on Ayurveda
Organization: World Health Organization (WHO)
URL: https://www.who.int/publications/b/55789

Key information:
WHO published international standard terminologies for Ayurveda.

Standard terminology can help professionals, researchers, policymakers and other users communicate consistently about Ayurveda.

The terminology resource supports international cooperation, information exchange, standards and classifications relating to Ayurveda.

SOURCE 5
Title: WIPO Treaty on Intellectual Property, Genetic Resources and Associated Traditional Knowledge
Organization: World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO)
URL: https://www.wipo.int/en/web/traditional-knowledge/wipo-treaty-on-ip-gr-and-associated-tk

Key information:
WIPO member states adopted the WIPO Treaty on Intellectual Property, Genetic Resources and Associated Traditional Knowledge in May 2024.

The treaty addresses the relationship between intellectual property, genetic resources and associated traditional knowledge.

The treaty includes provisions concerning patent applications involving genetic resources and associated traditional knowledge.

The treaty's implementation and entry into force depend on the applicable international legal requirements.

For international questions, the current status of the treaty should always be verified from official WIPO information.

SOURCE 6
Title: Documentation of Traditional Knowledge
Organization: World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO)
URL: https://www.wipo.int/en/web/traditional-knowledge/resources/tk-and-tces

Key information:
Documentation of traditional knowledge involves identifying, collecting, organizing, recording or registering traditional knowledge.

Documentation can have intellectual property implications.

Documentation may help preserve knowledge and can also have risks if sensitive knowledge is exposed or misused.

WIPO recommends considering objectives, risks and potential benefits when documenting traditional knowledge.

GENERAL GUIDANCE
================

IP-SAKTI Sahayak should provide informational and source-grounded guidance.

The system should not claim to be a lawyer, patent agent or government authority.

The system should not invent laws, regulations, patent sections, government policies or legal conclusions.

If the available knowledge is insufficient to answer a question, the system should clearly say that there is insufficient information and recommend checking the relevant official authority.

For Indian patent questions, official Intellectual Property India resources should be preferred.

For international traditional knowledge and intellectual property questions, WIPO resources should be preferred.

For Ayurveda terminology and traditional medicine context, WHO resources may be useful.
